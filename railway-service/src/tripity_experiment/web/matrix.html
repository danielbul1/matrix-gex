    How to use GEX

    Matrix shows where options dealers are likely to buy or sell to stay hedged, strike by strike. This page explains what the numbers mean, what each screen shows, and how traders commonly read them.

    What GEX is

    Market makers sell most of the options that traders buy. To stay neutral they hedge with the underlying, and how much they must buy or sell when price moves depends on the options' gamma.

    GEX (gamma exposure) estimates that hedging in dollars at each strike: gamma × open interest × 100 × spot² × 1%. Matrix assumes dealers are long the calls and short the puts, so calls count as positive and puts as negative.

    Positive and negative gamma

    • Positive gamma (Net GEX above zero): dealers sell into rallies and buy into dips. Moves tend to be damped and price tends to stay in a range.
    • Negative gamma (Net GEX below zero): dealers sell into drops and buy into rallies. Moves tend to be faster and can run further.

    GEX is built from open interest known at the open, so it describes how the day starts. Volume during the day shows where new positions are being added.

    The screens

    Strike Chart (home)

    One ticker and one or more expirations, added together. Type a ticker and press Search or Enter. The nearest date is chosen at first; the menu lists every open expiry, so you add the ones you want and × removes a date. The chart shows strikes within about 1% of spot for SPX and NDX, and about 3% for other tickers. The buttons turn layers on and off: Net GEX as bars, and AG, OI, volume and Power Zone as areas. Beside it are the day's 1-minute candles and a simplified price line. The candle chart carries the Price Lines of the chosen expirations.

    OI Volume

    Open interest and today's volume per strike for one expiration. Volume Spread is call volume minus put volume: blue where calls traded more, red where puts did. The price chart marks the strikes with the most call OI, put OI, call volume and put volume.

    GEX Analysis

    The board for SPX, SPY or QQQ over several expirations at once. Pick a ticker button; it starts on the nearest expiry. Click expiration buttons to add or remove dates, or use Select All and Clear All. Each expiration button shows its share of the ticker's gamma. The line above them gives spot, Total Gamma and the regime. It shows:

    • A table of every strike within 5% of spot. Click a column header to sort. A green dot marks each column's largest value, and its row is shaded.
    • Net Gamma: Net GEX per strike, with an Abs GEX line.
    • Gamma Split: call gamma above zero and put gamma below it, with an Abs GEX line.
    • A price chart with the Price Lines.

    The ⛶ button opens a chart full screen. ✕ or Esc closes it.

    Key Levels

    The four Key Levels of a ticker, drawn on five days of price, from every open expiration. Under the chart, the Forecast reads the same data as text.

    P/C Ratio

    Puts over calls for SPX, NDX, SPY and QQQ, across all expirations, by open interest and by volume. A ratio above 1 means more puts than calls.

    Using it when trading

    Direction for the day

    • Start with the regime: is the total Net GEX positive or negative, and which side of Zero Gamma is spot on?
    • In positive gamma, expect ranges: fades toward the Call Wall and Put Wall tend to work better than breakouts.
    • In negative gamma, expect larger moves: a break of a wall can keep going.

    Supply and demand zones

    • Large positive Net GEX and large AG strikes above price often act as supply (resistance); below price, as demand (support).
    • Mark them on your own chart and watch how price reacts when it reaches them.
    • Check OI Volume to see whether today's volume is adding to a level or moving away from it.

    0DTE

    • Pick today's expiration on the Strike Chart. Same-day options carry the most gamma, so their levels matter most during the session.
    • Turn on Power Zone and watch the Matrix level: price often gravitates toward it into the afternoon.
    • Data reloads only when you press Search or a button, so refresh before you act.

    Limits

    • Matrix assumes dealers are long calls and short puts. Real positioning can differ.
    • Open interest updates once a day, and prices may be delayed.
    • Levels describe where hedging is likely, not where price must go. Use them with your own analysis and risk management.
